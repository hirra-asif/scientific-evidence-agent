---
name: scientific-evidence
description: Assess scientific claims against research abstracts, checking population, study context, numerical findings and evidence sufficiency.
---

Use the corpus search and abstract-reading tools to assess the claim.

For each abstract:

1. Compare the exact population, intervention or exposure, outcome,
   direction of effect and qualifiers in the claim and abstract.

2. Distinguish human, animal and cell studies. Evidence from one
   population does not automatically establish a claim about another.

3. Preserve the study's wording about association and causation.
   Do not strengthen an association into a causal conclusion.

4. Use CONTRADICT only when the abstract provides findings incompatible
   with the claim. Missing information alone is NOT_ENOUGH_INFO.

5. Preserve reported numerical values and units. Avoid unnecessary
   derived ratios or conversions unless verified by calculation.

6. Cite the smallest set of sentences that together justify the verdict.
   Include a qualifying sentence only if leaving it out would change
   or overstate the verdict.
   Cite only sentences from abstracts you actually read.

7. Return the required structured report. For NOT_ENOUGH_INFO,
   use an empty evidence sentence list.
